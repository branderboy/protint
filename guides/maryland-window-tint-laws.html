The Numbers

MARYLAND TINT LIMITS BY VEHICLE TYPE

Maryland's rule is written around visible light transmission, or VLT. It is the share of light that makes it through the glass and the film together. The lower the number, the darker the window. Here is where the line is.

Passenger cars (sedans, coupes, hatchbacks, wagons)

  • Front door windows: at least 35% VLT
  • Rear door and quarter windows: at least 35% VLT
  • Rear windshield: at least 35% VLT
  • Windshield: non-reflective tint allowed only above the AS-1 line, usually the top five inches

Multipurpose vehicles (SUVs, pickups, minivans, vans)

  • Front door windows: at least 35% VLT
  • All windows behind the driver: any darkness
  • Windshield: same AS-1 line rule as cars

This is why factory privacy glass on a pickup or SUV is legal while the same shade on a sedan is not. The vehicle class on your registration decides which column you are in.

Reflectivity and color

Maryland also prohibits mirrored or highly reflective film and red or amber tints on windows. Every film we install is a neutral charcoal with low reflectivity, so this is never an issue with our work.

Medical exemptions

The Maryland MVA issues window tint exemptions for documented light-sensitive medical conditions. The paperwork comes from the state after a physician's certification, and it has to be in the car. We will install darker film for a customer who shows us a valid exemption. We cannot issue one.

Across the Line

WASHINGTON, DC TINT LIMITS

DC is stricter than Maryland on cars and about the same on trucks and SUVs. If your vehicle is registered in the District, these are the limits.

DC passenger cars

  • Front door windows: at least 70% VLT
  • Rear side windows: at least 50% VLT
  • Rear windshield: at least 50% VLT

DC trucks, vans and SUVs

  • Front door windows: at least 55% VLT
  • Rear side windows and rear windshield: at least 35% VLT

Our shops are in Maryland, but we tint plenty of DC-registered cars from Southeast and Northeast. Tell us where the car is registered when you book and we will stock the right shade for the appointment. A 70% film still blocks UV and cuts heat; it just does not look dark.

What Enforcement Looks Like

REPAIR ORDERS, INSPECTIONS AND WHAT THEY COST YOU

Tint enforcement in Prince George's County is real but not random. The two places it bites are traffic stops and the Maryland safety inspection.

On a traffic stop, an officer with a tint meter can issue a Safety Equipment Repair Order. You then have a short window to remove the film, have the car inspected and return the signed order. Miss it and the fine grows and your registration can be flagged.

On a safety inspection, which Maryland requires when a used vehicle is sold or brought in from another state, illegal tint is a failure item. The inspection station will not pass the car until the film is removed. That is where a lot of our removal customers come from: a car bought in Virginia or Florida with 20% all around.

Both situations end the same way, with a removal bill and a new install. Starting at 35% skips all of it.

This guide is a plain-language summary for drivers, not legal advice. The Maryland Transportation Article and the MVA's published window tint rules control if anything here is out of date.

Related Questions

QUICK ANSWERS

What is the darkest legal tint in Maryland?

35% visible light transmission on every window of a passenger car. On trucks, SUVs and vans, 35% on the front door windows, with no limit behind the driver.

Can I have 20% tint in Maryland?

Not on the front doors of any vehicle, and not on any window of a passenger car, without a medical exemption from the MVA. 20% is fine behind the driver on a truck, SUV or van.

Is 35% tint legal in DC?

Only on the rear windows of trucks, vans and SUVs. DC passenger cars are limited to 70% on the front sides and 50% on the rear. SUVs and vans are 55% front and 35% rear.

What happens if I get caught with illegal tint?

In Maryland, a police officer can issue a Safety Equipment Repair Order. You then have a set number of days to remove the film and have the vehicle inspected, or face a fine and possible registration suspension. Illegal tint also fails the Maryland safety inspection required when a used vehicle is sold or brought into the state.

Does Pro Tint install darker than legal if I ask?

Not on the front doors of any vehicle, and not on any window of a Maryland passenger car. We will install darker behind the driver on trucks and SUVs, where Maryland allows it.
