The Three Film Types

DYED, CARBON AND CERAMIC, SIDE BY SIDE

All automotive window film is a polyester sheet with adhesive on one side and a scratch-resistant coat on the other. What sits in the middle decides how it performs, how long it lasts and what it costs. There are three types that matter, and we install two of them.

Dyed film: what we do not install

Dyed film is the cheapest thing on the market and it is what you get from a $70 tint special. The dye absorbs visible light, so the window looks dark, but it blocks little heat and the dye breaks down under UV. In two or three Maryland summers it fades to purple and the adhesive starts to let go. We do not stock it, and if you have it on your car now, removal and carbon is the fix.

Carbon film: our standard

Our standard film is a carbon film from Global Window Films. Carbon particles in the sheet absorb a meaningful share of infrared and keep the color stable for life, which is why we can put a lifetime warranty against fading and purple on every car. It is a neutral charcoal with a slightly matte look and no metal, so phones, GPS and toll transponders work normally. This is the film included in the $108 car and $60 truck prices.

Ceramic film: the upgrade

Ceramic film adds a layer of ceramic nanoparticles tuned to absorb and reflect infrared. At the same 35% shade it blocks far more heat than carbon, and it does it without metal, so there is no signal interference and no mirror effect. It costs more per foot, so we quote it per vehicle. 3M film is also available on request for customers who prefer that brand.

Metallized film: also not offered

Older heat-rejecting films used a thin metal layer. They work, but they have a mirrored look Maryland limits, and they can weaken cell, GPS and E-ZPass signals. Ceramic made them obsolete.

Our Recommendation

WHICH ONE SHOULD YOU GET?

Get ceramic if the car parks outside at work, you drive a lot in summer, the interior is black, or you tried cheap tint before and felt no difference in heat. Get carbon if the car is garaged, your main goals are privacy and looks, or you are tinting a truck's two front windows to match the factory rear glass.

On the fence? Ceramic on the two front doors and carbon on the rest. The driver gets the heat benefit where it matters most and the rear glass gets the same look for less.

Shade is a separate decision from film type. A 70% ceramic blocks more heat than a 20% dyed film. Our tint percentage guide covers the darkness side.

Related Questions

QUICK ANSWERS

Which films does Pro Tint actually install?

Our standard film is a carbon film from Global Window Films, included in the $108 car and $60 truck prices. A ceramic upgrade is available on any vehicle and quoted per job. 3M film is available on request. We do not install plain dyed film.

Is ceramic tint worth it?

If the car parks outside, you commute in summer or the interior is black, most customers say yes. If the car is garaged and your goals are privacy and looks, carbon at the same shade does the job for less.

Does ceramic last longer than carbon?

Both carry a lifetime warranty against fading and color change, so the warranty is the same. Ceramic's heat rejection does not degrade over time. Plain dyed film is the one that fails.

Can you mix films on one car?

Yes. Ceramic on the front doors, where the driver feels the heat, and carbon on the rear is a common and sensible setup.

What does the heat-rejection spec actually mean?

Film makers publish total solar energy rejected (TSER) and infrared rejection (IRR). TSER is the honest number: the share of all the sun's energy the film stops. Ask any shop for TSER, not just IR, and compare at the same shade.
